What is Cyber Essentials?

Cyber Essentials is a certification scheme run by the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), the UK's technical authority on cyber security. Its delivery partner, IASME, licenses the Certification Bodies that assess and certify organisations. The scheme focuses on five technical controls that, when applied properly, help to protect against the majority of common, low-skill internet attacks.

Who is it for?

Any organisation of any size, from a sole trader to a large company, in any sector. It is particularly useful for small and medium-sized businesses that want a clear, achievable security baseline.

Why does it matter?

  • Required for many UK central government contracts that handle personal or sensitive information
  • Builds trust with customers and supply-chain partners
  • Viewed positively by many cyber insurers
  • Gives you a clear picture of your own security

How long does it last?

Both Cyber Essentials and Cyber Essentials Plus certificates are valid for 12 months. Organisations must re-certify every year to stay certified, because threats and IT systems change over time.

What changed in April 2026?

Assessments started on or after 27 April 2026 use version 3.3 of the Requirements for IT Infrastructure and the new Danzell question set, which replaced the previous Willow question set. The update clarifies scoping and multi-factor authentication expectations, and makes the 14-day patching questions automatic-fail questions.

The five technical controls

Every in-scope device, cloud service and network must meet all five controls. They are listed below in the order the requirements document presents them.

  1. Firewalls

    Use a correctly configured firewall to protect every device that connects to the internet. Change default administrative passwords on routers and block any inbound services that are not needed.

  2. Secure configuration

    Remove or disable unused software and accounts, change default passwords, and turn off features such as auto-run. Devices should only offer the services your organisation actually needs.

  3. Security update management

    Keep all software licensed and supported. High-risk and critical security updates must be installed within 14 days of release, and unsupported software must be removed from scope.

  4. User access control

    Give each person only the access they need for their job, use separate accounts for administrative tasks, and protect cloud services with multi-factor authentication wherever it is available.

  5. Malware protection

    Prevent malicious software from running by using anti-malware software or by only allowing approved applications to be installed and run.

How to get certified

There are two levels of certification. Most organisations start with Cyber Essentials and then move on to Cyber Essentials Plus if a customer or contract requires a higher level of assurance.

Cyber Essentials

Verified self-assessment

You answer an online question set about your IT. A qualified assessor from a Certification Body reviews your answers and, if they meet the requirements, issues your certificate.

Cyber Essentials Plus

Self-assessment plus technical audit

Includes everything in Cyber Essentials, followed by a hands-on technical audit of a sample of your systems by an assessor. You must hold a current Cyber Essentials certificate first.

Five steps to certification

  1. Define your scope. Decide which parts of your organisation, devices and cloud services will be assessed.
  2. Check your readiness. Compare your IT against the five controls and the current requirements document.
  3. Fix any gaps. Update software, remove unused accounts and switch on multi-factor authentication.
  4. Complete the assessment. Choose a Certification Body and submit the question set online.
  5. Receive and renew. Display your certificate and set a reminder to renew within 12 months.

How much does Cyber Essentials cost?

IASME Cyber Essentials assessment fees by organisation size (excluding VAT)
Organisation size Number of employees Fee
Micro 0 to 9 £320
Small 10 to 49 £440
Medium 50 to 249 £500
Large 250 or more £600

Fees are set by IASME and may change. Cyber Essentials Plus audit fees are set separately by each Certification Body and depend on the size of your organisation. Always check the latest prices before you apply.
